**INTERPRETATION.** Three things are confounded in every "rebuilt dry" result so far: (a) the note content (which notes, when), (b) the timbre and level of the source notes, (c) the search and score used. Nothing yet isolates (a), which is the only part the transcription contributes.

## 1. Is concatenative dry reconstruction from transcription sound?

**What the literature does.**

| Precedent | What it shows | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| CoSaRef (2024): MIDI-to-audio by note-level concatenation of library samples, then a diffusion refiner trained without MIDI pairs; beats a MIDI-supervised timbre-controllable baseline in objective and listening tests | Concatenation from MIDI is an accepted synthesis baseline, and its known weakness (flat timbre, no expression) is what the refiner fixes | FACT [arXiv 2410.16785](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.16785) |
| SynthTab (2023-24): 7,684 h of tablature rendered through Ample Sound virtual guitars via DawDreamer; techniques (hammer-on, pull-off, slide, palm mute, harmonics) via keyswitches, bends and vibrato via MIDI; pre-training helps GuitarSet and EGDB, but the authors report "significant challenges" generalising to unseen guitar data and name the sim-to-real "domain mismatch" | Rendered-from-symbolic guitar is useful but measurably not real guitar, even with a commercial sampler that handles techniques | FACT [arXiv 2309.09085](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.09085v4) |
| Every comparable effect-estimation paper renders **real** DI through plug-ins; none trains or searches on transcribe-and-resynthesise guitar | No published support for our rebuild as a search input | FACT (absence, within what was read) [T158 survey](no-di-survey-estimation.md) |
| Analysis-by-synthesis with unknown source in the effect literature is solved by making the loss content-tolerant (ST-ITO uses different content for input and reference; Chen et al. 2024 tone embedding), not by synthesising the source. ST-ITO's authors say it "does not work well" for guitar tone matching | The field's answer to "unknown dry" is a content-invariant metric or a dry-recovery model, not a rebuilt dry | FACT [ST-ITO](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.21233v1), [Chen 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.10646) |

**Failure modes, in the order I expect them to matter (INTERPRETATION unless marked).**

| Failure | Why it matters for an amp/drive search | Evidence we hold |
|---|---|---|
| Note density and timing | A drive/amp is level- and envelope-driven; the pick density sets how often the gain stage is hit and how the sustain overlaps. The chord rebuild (fewer, cleaner strikes) lost to the plain one by ear | DECISIONS 2026-10-22; [T203](../../board/TASKS.md#t203 "Install Spotify Basic Pitch locally") "the plain one keeps the picking density" |
| Dynamics (velocity) | Basic Pitch velocity is a posterior amplitude, not a pick strength; [T201](../../board/TASKS.md#t201 "Rebuild a dry DI track for Mother from the Basic Pitch transcription…") maps it linearly to gain. Input level into the drive is the Drive confound ([T194](../../board/TASKS.md#t194 "Explain the Drive anomaly")) | [T194](drive-anomaly-t194.md) |
| Palm mutes and technique | Palm-muted notes have a different spectrum and decay; the [T041](../../board/TASKS.md#t041 "End-to-end on a real song") palm-muted DI tripled the preset loss. No technique labels in the transcription | [T041](../../board/TASKS.md#t041), [T201](rebuilt-dry-t201.md) section "Playing technique" |
| Chords as summed single notes | Real power chords beat and intermodulate inside the clipper; summed isolated notes do not, so the rebuilt chords distort differently. The chord rebuild did not fix this (still summed single notes) | [T205](chord-rebuild-t205.md) |
| Register/octave errors | Octave-tolerant F is 0.05 above exact ([T203](../../board/TASKS.md#t203 "Install Spotify Basic Pitch locally")), so some notes are an octave off; an octave down through a drive changes low-end saturation and the cab's response | [T203](basic-pitch-t203.md) |
| String/position | Same pitch on a different string differs in brightness and sustain; `--prefer-position low` moved the alignment score 0.286 to 0.320 with identical notes, so this is audible to the metric | [T203](basic-pitch-t203.md) |
| Separator residue | Transcription runs on a stem with about 20 dB less bass and bleed; missed low notes and spurious partials are built in | [T203](basic-pitch-t203.md) limits |

**Verdict (INTERPRETATION).** The rebuild is a defensible *level-and-rhythm* proxy, and the literature's nearest analogue (CoSaRef, SynthTab) says concatenation is a reasonable baseline that is still measurably not the real instrument. It is not a sound *timbre* proxy: everything that makes a drive sound like itself (dynamics, technique, chord intermodulation) is the part the rebuild does not carry. Whether that matters for *ranking* candidates is exactly the un-run ablation (section 7, item 1). Until then the rebuilt dry's contribution is a hypothesis.

**Could a recovered pseudo-dry feed our renderer? (INTERPRETATION)**
- Through a Helix drive+amp+cab the distortion is not memoryless and not one plug-in family; every supervised remover above is in-domain to one vendor and none has seen a separated stem. Expect worse than their 24-29 dB SI-SDR; the MSR guitar numbers (about 4 dB Multi-Mel-SNR from a mastered mix) are the realistic ceiling for stem-plus-effects today.
- A pseudo-dry with 5-10 dB SI-SDR fed to a drive would re-distort the remover's artefacts; the gain stage amplifies exactly the residual. That is the tolerance sweep the [T158](../../board/TASKS.md#t158 "No-DI method survey (human 2026-10-18") survey proposed (option 4) and is still the right first test before any remover is wired in.
- **The hybrid is more promising than either alone:** pitch and timing from the transcription (what Basic Pitch is decent at), envelope and dynamics from the stem or a pseudo-dry (what the rebuild lacks). Concretely: impose the stem's note-level RMS envelope (per onset, smoothed) on the concatenated notes instead of Basic Pitch velocity. Cost: a few hours of code, no training, no new licence. This is a method change and needs the human's approval.

**Reading (INTERPRETATION).**
- On clean guitar the best 2024-25 models are about 8-10 F-points above Basic Pitch; on EGDB electric tones TART reaches 0.78. None reports numbers on **separated stems of distorted guitar in a mix**, which is where our 0.30 comes from; most of our loss is the stem, not the model. A 10-point gain on clean audio will not become 10 points on our stems.
- String/fret labels are only useful if the note bank has per-string notes to draw from. The prs-7-tone bank has string and fret labels ([T204](../../board/TASKS.md#t204 "Guitar calibration (human 2026-10-22)") segments a, b), Guitar-TECHS has them from the MIDI track, so a tab model's string choice *could* pick a same-string sample. The [T203](../../board/TASKS.md#t203 "Install Spotify Basic Pitch locally") low-position result (0.286 to 0.320 alignment from sample choice alone) says string choice is audible. But tab accuracy is 42% on symbolic data (TART) and physically-constrained low-position heuristics already get most of the way for riff material; a learned tab model is not the first thing to buy.
- Cheapest transcription upgrade: run TART or the domain-adapted model (if obtainable) on the human's **clean takes** and on the stems, scored the same way as [T203](../../board/TASKS.md#t203 "Install Spotify Basic Pitch locally"). If the stem score does not move by more than the 0.04 the [T203](../../board/TASKS.md#t203 "Install Spotify Basic Pitch locally") sweep found, transcription quality is not the bottleneck and the effort goes elsewhere.

**INTERPRETATION.** The own-take route is the only one with a measured accuracy gain in this repo, and it costs the user 2-3 minutes per song. The predictor route is the only one that is instant and needs no dry; its gap is new-player generalisation and chains. The embedding and differential routes are unproven here and should be gated by the invariance-ratio check before any renders are spent.

MVSep's figures are on its own validation set, measured by MVSep, not by us; real-song quality here is still unmeasured (separation-models.md). A 4 dB SDR step (5.2 to 9.05) is large, but the only checkpoint that delivers it has no licence grant.

**Separation-consistent comparison vs training on separated audio (INTERPRETATION).**
- *Separation-consistent scoring* (render candidate, mix into the backing, re-separate, compare stem to stem) puts both sides through the same artefact generator, so the separator's bass loss and hiss cancel to first order. Cost: one separation per candidate (htdemucs_6s is seconds per 10 s clip on MPS), i.e. it fits a shortlist re-score of 10-50 candidates, not a 344-render grid. It needs the backing (the song minus the guitar stem, which we already have as the other stems). No literature found that does this for effect matching; StemFX trains on pseudo-stems, which is the training-side version.
- *Training on separated audio* fixes the predictor's input distribution once and costs nothing at run time; it is the right fix for the predictor route (section 4). The two are complementary: scoring-side consistency for search, training-side for the predictor.
- The cheapest discriminating test is the scoring one: take the 7 [T211](../../board/TASKS.md#t211 "All seven songs with the calibrated guitar") picks plus their 24-neighbour shortlists, re-score separation-consistently, and see whether the ordering changes and whether the human's "pretty off" songs (teen-spirit, seek-destroy) move. About 200 separations, under an hour, no training.

| Product | What it does about level | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Neural Amp Modeler | Captures carry `input_level_dbu` / `output_level_dbu` metadata; the user measures their interface with a 1 kHz sine and a voltmeter; the plug-in then auto-adjusts input gain so gain staging matches the analog gear | FACT [NAM calibration docs](https://neural-amp-modeler.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tutorials/calibration.html) |

| Kemper | "Clean Sens" is a per-guitar input sensitivity that balances clean against distorted level; the forum consensus is that the input is forgiving | UNVERIFIED (forum snippets) [kemper forum](https://forum.kemper-amps.com/forum/thread/32986-clean-sens-setup) |
| Our pipeline | Old renders normalised to -26.7 dBFS; new renders raw; the head reads Drive about 0.01 per dB wrong when the level convention changes; the human's own level is -28.1 (play-along) or -36.1 (calibration) | FACT [T194](drive-anomaly-t194.md), [T204](guitar-profile-t204.md) |

**What this means for us (INTERPRETATION).**
1. The level the preset will be *played at* is the play-along level (-28.1 dBFS here), because that is the guitar into the Stomp's input with the user's real pickup setting; the calibration session's -36.1 dBFS is 8 dB lower for an unknown reason (knob, pickup, input gain, or pick strength on single notes vs riffs). The profile should store a level **measured on the free-playing segment g** and on the riff-like segment e, and warn when they disagree with a play-along take by more than 3 dB. This is a measurement rule, not a model decision, but still needs the human.
2. Normalising both to a reference removes the information. The NAM approach is the right one: carry the level as metadata, keep the DI raw, and let the head and the search see it ([T194](../../board/TASKS.md#t194 "Explain the Drive anomaly") v2cond already shows the head is unbiased when told the level). For the renderer: the rebuilt dry should be at the play-along level, and the preset's Input gain left at 0.
3. Drive and level stay confounded in no-DI mode by physics; the honest output there is "Drive at the stated input level", as [T194](../../board/TASKS.md#t194 "Explain the Drive anomaly") proposed.
