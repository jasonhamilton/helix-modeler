## 4. The two technical questions

### 4.1 Why a cab after drive+amp erases the single-block heads

- **Facts.** The cab head after the cab is already down to 0.33 (it never sees a raw DI in training either); the cab head is nearly indifferent to the amp in front; drive and amp heads fall to chance after the cab [R [T214](../../board/TASKS.md#t214 "Chain transfer test (human 2026-10-23 \"yes\")")]. AFx-Rep is a Cnn14 on log-mel with a global pooled 512-d output (afx-rep.md).
- **Interpretation.** A Helix cab is an LTI filter with 20–40 dB of attenuation above about 5 kHz and deep comb structure [UNVERIFIED magnitude; the [T117](../../board/TASKS.md#t117 "Linear specialist for cab and EQ") linear tables hold the measured curves]. Three things follow. (i) The single-block amp and drive heads were trained on **cab-less** audio, where model identity is carried largely by fizz, aliasing and harmonic texture above 5 kHz; the cab removes exactly that band, so the features move far outside the training manifold, and the heads collapse onto one class (the "Sub Oct Fuzz / Kinetic" pile-ups in [T214](../../board/TASKS.md#t214 "Chain transfer test (human 2026-10-23 \"yes\")") are the signature of out-of-distribution inputs). (ii) A globally pooled log-mel embedding is dominated by the long-term spectral envelope, and the cab is the largest envelope change in the chain, so the embedding of any chain is "mostly cab". That is why the cab head survives and why cab top-3 is 0.40. (iii) Upstream blocks also hurt (a drive in front of the amp: 0.60 → 0.16) because a drive changes the amp's input level and spectrum, which is the hidden-variable problem already noted for input level (predictor-design.md section 1).
- **Which methods are robust to it.** (a) and (b): training on chain renders puts the cab inside the training manifold; conditional decoding exploits the fact that the cab is the most identifiable block. (e): contrastive training with positives spanning different DIs **and** the same amp under different cabs would force the embedding to look past the envelope, but that is a different positive definition from "same chain", and it discards cab information needed elsewhere; use two heads, not one embedding (INTERPRETATION). A cheaper feature-level fix to test on existing data: per-clip spectral whitening (divide the log-mel by its own time-average, or cepstral liftering) before the heads, so that what remains is texture and dynamics rather than envelope. It will cost cab and EQ accuracy and may win amp/drive accuracy; measure both on the [T214](../../board/TASKS.md#t214 "Chain transfer test (human 2026-10-23 \"yes\")") features (zero renders). Methods **not** robust: any single-block head used as is ([T214](../../board/TASKS.md#t214 "Chain transfer test (human 2026-10-23 \"yes\")")); LTAS-type scores; retrieval in a raw log-mel space.
