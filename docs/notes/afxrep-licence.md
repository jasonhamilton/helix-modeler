### Training data

- **Paper:** seven datasets (MTG-Jamendo, ENST-Drums, URSing, FSD50k, LibriSpeech, Medley-solos-DB, GuitarSet), ~60 h, ~20,000 examples each ~11 s at 48 kHz, rendered through **63 "open source or freely available VST3 audio plugins compiled for Linux"** with random presets; pretext task is effect classification plus preset prediction; 1 M steps, batch 32 ([paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.21233v1)). MUSDB18 appears in the paper only as an evaluation set.
- **Shipped `config.yaml`** (local copy `cache/models/afx-rep/config.yaml`, lines 130-138) lists **eight** training tarfiles: the seven above **plus `musdb18-vst3-presets.tar`**, and validates on GuitarSet. The paper and the config disagree on MUSDB18. FACT that the config lists it; whether the released checkpoint was actually trained with it is **unverified**. Treat it as included.
- The 63 plugins are not named on the card; the paper names TubeScreamer, ZamEQ2, ZamCompX2, ZamDelay and TAL-Reverb-4 only. Plugin licences (some Zam/TAL-style plugins are GPL, UNVERIFIED) are a second unknown, though the plugins only processed audio and I know of no rule that makes the output audio carry the plugin licence (INTERPRETATION).

### Do those terms reach a trained checkpoint, heads and fine-tunes?

- **FACT:** nothing in the "apache-2.0" tag or the card mentions the data terms. Apache-2.0 §4 (redistribution) requires a copy of the licence, notice of changes to files you modify, and retained notices ([apache.org/licenses/LICENSE-2.0](https://www.apache.org/licenses/LICENSE-2.0)); it says nothing about upstream data.
- **FACT:** ENST-Drums Art. 7-3 forbids creating a "derivate work of the databases" without consent, and 7-2 forbids commercial use. Whether a rendered-and-embedded training run makes a classifier a derivative work of the *database* is the open question.
- **INTERPRETATION (unsettled law):** whether weights are a derivative of their training data is unresolved; the common cautious practice is to keep NC-licensed data out of models meant for commercial use ([LWN summary](https://lwn.net/Articles/964739), [HF discussion on weight licences](https://huggingface.co/espnet/xeus/discussions/4); both seen as search results, not read in full, UNVERIFIED). The Creative Commons position on AI training is at [creativecommons.org/using-cc](https://creativecommons.org/using-cc) (not read). I did not research EU/UK/US text-and-data-mining exceptions, which could change this for research use; a lawyer should.
- **INTERPRETATION, by artefact:**
  - *Heads trained on frozen AFx-Rep embeddings* (logistic regression, MLP on 512-d vectors): contain none of AFx-Rep's weights, are trained on our labels and our Native or Stomp renders. Weakest link to the upstream data. They are, however, useless without AFx-Rep, so a published head implies "needs AFx-Rep".
  - *Fine-tunes of the encoder:* contain modified AFx-Rep weights, so Apache-2.0 §4 applies and the upstream-data question is carried in full. Highest risk of the three.
  - *Distilling AFx-Rep into a new model* (train a student on AFx-Rep's outputs, no weight copying): still derived from AFx-Rep's behaviour. Weaker link than fine-tuning, not nil (INTERPRETATION).

## 2. Practical consequences

(a) **Runtime download on the user's machine.** FACT: the project redistributes nothing; the user pulls the file from the uploader's HF repo under its stated Apache-2.0 tag, and the code is pinned and hash-checked. INTERPRETATION: lowest-risk case; the user is the one using the model and upstream NC terms bind data licensees, not downstream model users. Keep it that way: do not mirror, bundle, or auto-download from a project server; add the data-terms caveat to the user-facing docs. Residual risk is on the uploader, not on us.

(b) **Publishing heads trained on AFx-Rep embeddings.** INTERPRETATION: probably permissible under the human's 2026-10-17 ruling (weights trained on Native renders may be published; the entry I found is at `docs/DECISIONS.md` line 798, dated 2026-10-17; I did not locate the 2026-10-18 entry on third-party base-weight licences) and the heads hold no AFx-Rep weights. Conditions I would put on the model card: names AFx-Rep, its tag and its revision/hash; states the training-data terms are unresolved; says the heads need the user to fetch the base checkpoint; separate note for the Line 6 Native-render licence question already in `line6-eula-flags.md`. Do **not** publish `encoder.pt`, a fine-tune, or any file containing AFx-Rep tensors without a ruling.

(c) **Shipping in a commercial product.** FACT: Apache-2.0 code and the tag permit commercial use. FACT: the data licences above forbid commercial use for their licensees. INTERPRETATION: this is a real, unquantified risk that no licence text in the chain resolves, concentrated on ENST-Drums (explicit no-derivative and no-commercial clauses) and MTG-Jamendo. A commercial product should not bundle AFx-Rep or fine-tunes of it without either a lawyer's opinion or written confirmation from the author, or should move to a licence-clean encoder (section 3). A product that downloads AFx-Rep at runtime from the original HF repo on the user's behalf sits closer to (a) but is still the vendor's use in a commercial context (INTERPRETATION).

## 3. Alternatives

The existing hook is `Embedder(model=...)` / `set_embedder` in `afxrep.py`: any torch module with `model(x: (b, chs, n)) -> (mid, side)` works, so most candidates are a wrapper plus resampling and a pooled feature. Cheapest test per candidate is below. Caveat on tests: [T185](nodi-drive-e2e-v2.md)'s per-job JSON is kept (4 MB) but its target audio was deleted and the renders may not be retained (UNVERIFIED; I did not read `scripts/nodi_drive_e2e_v2.py`), so the cheapest comparison is the labelled-clip harness of [T186](predictor-pilot-v0.md) (8,711 training clips, AFx-Rep + logreg: top-1 0.56 / top-5 0.90 / family 0.65; cross-player 0.42 / 0.79 / 0.51), then [T185](../../board/TASKS.md#t185 "Larger no-DI drive e2e measurement") blind top-1 (AFx 37/96 = 39%) only for a candidate that matches on [T186](../../board/TASKS.md#t186 "Predictor pilot v0, drive only, on renders on disk"). Embedding ~8.7k 10 s clips costs about 0.065 s each for a Cnn14-class CPU model (afx-rep.md), so roughly 10 minutes plus loading. Do not run it from this task.

## 4. Ranked recommendation (for the human; nothing decided)

1. **Test PANNs Cnn14 frozen + the same heads on the [T186](../../board/TASKS.md#t186 "Predictor pilot v0, drive only, on renders on disk") harness first.** Weights are CC BY 4.0, the architecture is already vendored, and it costs about 10 minutes of CPU. If it lands near AFx-Rep (0.56 top-1), it replaces AFx-Rep for anything published or commercial and the legal question disappears for the base weights. If it is clearly worse, that tells us how much AFx-Rep's effect-specific pretraining is worth. DAC (MIT) is the same-cost second test.
2. **Train our own contrastive "same setting, different guitar" encoder**, initialised from PANNs (permissive) or from scratch, on Native renders across many DIs. It is the best fit and the cleanest provenance, but it costs render time and DI diversity ([T156](../../board/TASKS.md#t156 "Grow the DI library before Gate 3") says unused DI passages are nearly exhausted), and it carries the Line 6 Native-render question already ruled on for publishing weights.
3. **Keep AFx-Rep as the local baseline and for (a) runtime use**, with a notice in the user docs. Publish heads trained on it only with the model-card conditions in section 2(b) and after the human accepts the unresolved data-terms risk. Do not publish AFx-Rep fine-tunes or `encoder.pt`; do not use it in a commercial bundle without a lawyer or author confirmation.
4. **Open-Amp-style encoder retrained by us**: same direction as 2, small and fast, but the original has no weights and its data inputs are not clean either (CC BY-NC-ND guitar data); only as a recipe.
5. **Avoid** Fx-Encoder++ and RelFx weights (NC, share-alike), FXencoder_ps (unknown), EnCodec until its weights licence is found, wav2vec2 (wrong domain).

Questions for the human: (1) is "runtime download, local use" enough for AFx-Rep in the next release, or must the shipped path be licence-clean? (2) is the data-terms risk acceptable for published heads? (3) should the author (Christian Steinmetz) be asked in writing what data the checkpoint saw and whether the apache-2.0 tag was meant to cover it? Item 3 is cheap and could resolve most of the ambiguity.
