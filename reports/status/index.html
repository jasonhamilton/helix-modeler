Executive dashboard

What we are building. Give the tool a song and get back a preset for the Line 6 HX Stomp guitar pedal that sounds like the guitar on the recording, checked on the real pedal. It works by trying candidate presets, comparing the sound with the recording, and adjusting until they match.

Summary

The goal is a tool that turns a song into an HX Stomp preset that sounds like the recorded guitar. This week the first blind listening on real songs came back negative: the human said the drive-only matches were so poor they could not even be compared, although earlier open listens called them pretty good. The human then narrowed the product to a drive effect, an equaliser and a light reverb, with no amp, cabinet or delay. A light reverb is preferred by ear, but nothing measured from the recording picks it reliably: one song confirmed a reverb estimator and one did not. Biggest problem: Nothing measured from the recording reliably sets the drive knobs, and the first blind test of drive-only matches was negative

High Confirmed Problem

What is wrong
On 25 October the human heard, blind, the five best drive candidates for each of five songs against the separated guitar and said 'only 23 comes even close. That was pretty bad all around', then 'it's so bad I can't even compare them'. This is the first blind listening on real songs and it is negative (evidence class C as recorded by the lead: heard blind, answered aloud, not scored). It contradicts the open listen of 22 October that called the seven matches 'pretty good'. The dry-signal ablation then showed the winning drive model barely depends on the clean guitar fed to the search: against the plain rebuild, 5 to 6 of 7 songs keep the same model under each alternative input, one song changes model in every arm, and the distances are not comparable across inputs. The listening measure that ranks the candidates (AFx-Rep) has no ear validation against a separated guitar, and its validation session produced a verdict instead of ranks.
Why it matters
The product promises a preset that sounds like the recorded guitar, and the first blind listening on real songs said the current drive-only matches do not. Reverb, the next stage the human wants, has no usable signal either. Until a model tied to what the human hears beats a sealed baseline and passes a blind listen, the tool cannot know whether a candidate is closer, so more search or tuning would be steering by an instrument the ear has not validated. The open bet is a predictor trained on drive, equaliser and reverb chains; it is still rendering its data.
What it prevents
A drive-only match cannot be shown to beat anything the human would accept, and the ranking and knobs rest on a measure the ear has not validated on real songs. Without a signal that tracks what the human hears, neither search nor prediction can be tuned toward a better answer.
What resolves it
Predict the product chain with a model trained on chain-rendered audio and judge it against the sealed set and a blind listen, with bars the human approves first; rate the built dry-ablation packs; treat the stem verdict as a standing negative until a blind session goes the other way.
Next step
Read the chain predictor when training ends; the human rates the dry-ablation packs and rules on the Gate 3 bars.

Reverb has no model: nothing measured from the recording agrees with the listener, and a real tail is readable on only 2 of 7 stems

High Confirmed Problem

Problem
The human rejected a fixed reverb default and asked for a model. The pre-registered pilot (27 reverb candidates per song, blind ranks on six songs, the listener consistent: mean rank difference 0.33 against 2.67 by chance) found no agreement from any computable signal: the listening measure 0.05, tail statistics 0.04, the time-based regressors -0.09, dip fill -0.40 (opposite sign). A blind estimator built afterwards is accurate on known reverbs (decay ordering 36 of 40 triples, rank correlation 0.85 to 0.89 on five songs; mix 0.88 to 0.94) but cannot tell Room from Plate from Hall (43 percent against 33 by chance), and on the real stems it reads a real tail on only 2 of 7 (Mother 0.70 s, Territorial 0.90 s); the other five read the separator's leftover. The pre-registered confirmation held on Mother (named closest ranked 1 of 7, named furthest 7 of 7) and was not reproducible on Territorial (the human's repeat got ranks 4 and 1), so pooled it did not confirm.
Impact
The product chain needs a reverb per song. The only evidence that reverb helps is open listening (a light reverb preferred on all seven songs); type and amount cannot be chosen from the stem on 5 of 7 songs, and Plate, the open favourite, was not preferred in the blind ranks (mean rank Room 3.9, Hall 4.0, Plate 4.4; ties frequent).

Known in part: separator leftovers hide the tail on 5 of 7 stems and the human said not to work on the separator; the shared low and high cuts remove most of what tells reverb types apart; some clips are hard to rank (Territorial repeat inconsistent; the Cherub Rock section was unsuitable).
Resolution
The human rules on 'estimate when readable, fall back otherwise'; the overnight reverb heads are compared with the estimator on the seven songs; more songs are ranked blind with the estimator's pick named in advance.

Problem

Features

Helix Native engine · ◕ working unit-tests hardware

Purpose. Render a DI clip through a candidate preset offline in Helix Native, fast enough to be the searching engine.

Done when. A NativeEngine behind the frozen interface renders any chain structure the search needs at a measured cost, loads candidates without a GUI step per candidate, agrees with the Stomp within a measured null residual, and has run a full synthetic recovery pass.

Functionality today (partial). Loads an arbitrary generated .hlx into Native with no human (in-process file drop, about 66 s per new chain structure, once per machine; needs TONEMATCH_NATIVE_AUTOCAPTURE=1 and a logged-in GUI session), drives parameters through a MIDI-CC map, and renders a 2 s clip in about 0.28 s with memory flat near 3.0 GB. Renders are history-independent and match each file's own capture at -317 dB. Mono in and out only; chains with an enabled delay or reverb need a 3 s pre-roll; Captures of new single-block chains take 0.22 s each when batched across three sessions with the parameter checks off (ledger 2026-10-16T00:58:52+00:00 (2 rows)), and a pool of 6 hosts renders and scores about 10 two-second tests per second. Windows and Linux are untested. Single-block templates for 605 pedal models load by model id with no capture (ledger 2026-10-16T15:25:12+00:00 (3 rows)). Verified on 2026-10-17: settings land exactly apart from recorded misses, and renders repeat to -161.5 dB.

Progress note. Hands-off loading (file drop plus MIDI-CC map), state-based reset renders (0.28 s, bit-identical to dedicated captures at -317 dB, flat 3.0 GB), stale-template protection and a memory watchdog are built, hardware-measured on one Mac, and exercised by a 449-render real-song search. Block-serial rendering is shown to equal one instance above 20 Hz (-67 to -79 dB). Still missing: Windows and Linux (the drop route is macOS VST3 only), stereo and dual-cab paths in the host, probe fingerprints, and any full synthetic recovery pass. Quality of what Native renders versus the pedal is only partly in parity (see evaluation), and nothing it renders has been blind-tested by ear. Phase 3 is finished (T055, T064): capture takes 1.2 s per chain in one session and 0.39 s with three, instead of 31 s (ledger 2026-10-15T23:57:11+00:00 (4 rows)); a render pool of 6 hosts (the cap the human approved on 2026-10-16) runs 9.9 two-second tests per second and 4.6 ten-second tests per second, each test being a render plus its score (ledger 2026-10-16T00:38:02+00:00); block bypass by CC matches separate captures at -328.7 dB (ledger 2026-10-16T00:29:34+00:00); a rejected model load is detected and closed; about 1 host in 10 that rendered differently is replaced by a start-up check; templates B and B0 are re-captured at the frozen values (ledger 2026-10-16T00:50:49+00:00 (2 rows)); and a scan of 607 Stomp block models found 2 that Native 3.82 refuses (ledger 2026-10-16T00:58:52+00:00 (2 rows)). Challenges and findings

  • Direct measurement found no delay or reverb on any song in the stems and could not have seen a light one: the rebuilt dry is not the real performance (T228). [docs/notes/colour-pilot-t228.md]
  • On five of seven stems the 'tail' is the separator's residue (-10 to -13 dB floor, same curve as the full mix); the human said not to work on the separator (2026-10-25). [docs/notes/reverb-estimator-t231.md]
  • Reverb type (Room, Plate, Hall) is not separable by the estimator, and the human's blind ranks did not prefer Plate. [docs/notes/reverb-pilot-t230.md]

Open blockers

  • No reverb model: nothing computed from the stems agreed with the human's reverb ranks, the estimator reads a real tail on only 2 of 7 stems (the rest is separator residue), and the confirmation held on one song of two. The human rejected a fixed default and asked for a model.

Decisions

  • Chain is drive -> EQ -> reverb; no amp or cab; delay on hold (human, DECISIONS 2026-10-25)
  • EQ options stay in the chain; a fixed reverb default is rejected; a reverb model is required (human, DECISIONS 2026-10-25)
  • No separator work; the stems stay as they are (human, DECISIONS 2026-10-25)

Risks

  • Light reverb was preferred in open listening on all seven songs, but not blind; the preference may be for 'more ambience', not for the song's own reverb.

Glossary

AFx-Rep
A learned listening measure (a neural network embedding) used here to order candidate presets by how close they sound to the target. Lower distance is closer.
approved-methods file
A list in the repository of method settings the human has ruled on, with the human's words; a test fails if the code's defaults drift from it.
Basic Pitch
A free, Apache-2.0 licensed program from Spotify that writes down the notes in a recording. Used here to turn a separated guitar part into notes, which are then played back as a clean guitar.
blind A/B
A listening test where the listener does not know which option is which.
blind reverb estimator
A calculation that reads a reverb's decay and amount from the tails left at the end of notes in a recording, without knowing the reverb setting.
block
One element in a preset's chain: an amp, cabinet, drive pedal, delay and so on.
block library
A stored set of single-block Native setups, one per pedal effect model, used to build and render candidate presets quickly.
bootstrap interval
A range for a measured difference, built by re-sampling the 96 items many times. If the whole range sits on the good side of a line, the difference is reliable at the stated confidence.
capture
The one-off step that teaches Helix Native a new chain: the preset file is loaded into its editor and the resulting state is recorded.
chain builder
The planned stage that decides which kinds of block a tone needs, orders them by common patterns learned from real presets, and then fills each slot using the single-block methods.
class C / class B
Evidence classes from the audit: C is an open listen (the listener knew which was which); B is blind on an unsealed set. See 'evidence class'.
CMA-ES
An optimisation algorithm used to search for suitable settings.
CoreAudio
The Mac's audio system. When it fails, no sound can be sent to or recorded from the pedal.
dB
Decibel, a logarithmic loudness or level-difference scale.
dBFS
Decibels relative to full scale: how loud a digital signal is. 0 is the loudest possible; -36 is much quieter than -28.
DI
Direct Input: a clean, unprocessed guitar recording, before any amp or effects.
different dry tracks
Two runs whose clean-guitar input differs (other notes, tone or level). A distance between their renders and the target mixes the effect of the guitar with the effect of the preset, so it cannot say which preset is closer.
DSP
Digital Signal Processing: the audio calculations inside amps and effects.
Early Reflections
A cabinet setting that adds the first short echoes of the room.
effect family
A group of effects that behave alike: gain (drive, fuzz, amp), linear (cabinet, equaliser), dynamics (compressor, gate), time-based (delay, reverb), modulation (chorus, flanger, phaser, tremolo).
EQ
Equaliser: a tone control that boosts or cuts parts of the frequency spectrum, such as bass or treble.
EULA
The software licence agreement shown when installing a product.
evidence class
How strong a claim's evidence is, from the audit: A blind with a sealed set and a bar registered first; B blind on an unsealed set; C an open listen by the human (knowing which is which); D numbers only, on an unadmitted measure or without a bar; E no evidence.
fine-tuning (knobs)
After the block model is chosen, a local search that adjusts only its knobs to bring the render closer to the target.
firmware
The software running inside the pedal.
fw
Short for firmware, the pedal's built-in software. The pedal was on version 3.50 until 2026-10-15 and is now on 3.80.
gate
A milestone with a written checklist and a human sign-off.
golden file
A reference preset file the owner exported from HX Edit or Native, used to check that our own files follow the same layout.
guitar profile
A one-off calibration of one guitar: its normal playing level, its tone compared with the library guitars, and a bank of its own notes.
held-out
Test items not used when tuning, so the result is not flattered by tuning.
Helix Native
Line 6's software version of the Helix sounds; it runs as a plugin and renders audio offline.
Hum and Ripple
Two amp settings that add a mains-hum style noise. The noise runs freely, so it differs between renders of the same preset.
HX Edit
Line 6's editing program for the pedal; it also imports preset files.
HX Stomp
The Line 6 guitar-effects pedal this project targets.
impulse response (IR)
A short recording of a speaker cabinet and microphone that can reproduce that cabinet's sound by convolution. A cabinet block in the pedal uses one.
invariance ratio
How much a listening measure moves with a change of clean guitar compared with a knob move. Above 1 means the guitar matters more than the knob; the learned measure scored 30 for small knob moves.
known-answer set
A test set made by rendering presets we already know through Helix, so we can check whether the tool recovers them.
ledger
The project's append-only record of measured runs; a claim without a row did not happen.
look-alike
A candidate preset that differs slightly from the true one (a nearby setting or a neighbouring model); used to test whether a measure can tell near matches apart.
loss
The score the optimiser tries to lower: how different two sounds are. Lower is a closer match.
LUFS
A loudness measure that follows how loud something sounds. Listening comparisons are level-matched with it so louder does not win.
middle baseline
A baseline that sets every knob to the middle of its range and picks nothing cleverly. It shows what a do-nothing guess scores.
MIDI CC
A control message that moves one knob or switch from software.
new-player generalisation
How well a trained predictor works on a guitarist whose playing it never saw in training. The honest test for a tool that will be used by someone else.
null residual
What is left when one recording is subtracted from another that should match. Reported in decibels below the signal; the more negative, the closer.
numeric mode
Helix Native starts each session in one of two slightly different rounding modes. The difference is tiny (rounding level) but can break exact cancellation tests.
open listen
A listening session where the listener knows which candidate is which. It can flatter a method; a blind session cannot.
P3
The third library guitarist's clean recordings, held back from every training set and every search so a sealed test can use them.
parity
Agreement between the two engines: the same preset is rendered on both and the recordings are compared.
Path A and Path B
The two signal paths of the HX Stomp. Using both at once renders two chains in one pass.
pre-registered
The analysis and the reading of its result were written down, with a hash, before any answer was seen, so the result cannot be bent to fit.
pre-roll
Extra audio played before the part that is recorded, so delays and reverbs start from the same state every time.
predictor
A trained program that looks at a sound and guesses which block model and settings made it. Used to narrow the search.
preset
A saved sound: a chain of amp, cabinet and effect blocks with their settings (an .hlx file).
product chain
The chain the product builds, as redefined by the human on 2026-10-25: a drive effect, then an equaliser, then a reverb. No amp, cabinet or delay for now.
provisional loss
The early combination of three tone measures used before any measure was admitted. Never admitted; at chance on hard pairs; still the default re-score of the block matcher.
random walk
A baseline search that tries 300 random settings and keeps the best, judged by the same trusted score. It scores high here because the tool has only 300 tries over a pool of 47 effect models and the score is a good judge, so even random guessing often lands on the right model. Earlier notes called it random search.
rank-1 rate
How often the true preset is ranked first among the candidates (the look-alikes). Higher is better; guessing among 20 gives 0.05.
readback
Reading the pedal's settings back after writing them to confirm they match.
rebuilt dry track
A stand-in clean guitar made for a song that has none: the notes found in the separated guitar are replayed using real clean guitar notes (from a library or from the player's own calibration session).
render
Play a test signal through a preset and record the audio that comes out.
render pool
Several copies of Helix Native running at once, each rendering a different candidate, to finish a batch sooner.
reverb
An effect that adds the sound of a space (room, plate, hall) after the guitar. Decay is how long the tail lasts; Mix is how much of it is blended in.
sealed set
A test set generated after the bars are registered, with hashes recorded, that no agent reads before the one scoring run.
search test
One candidate setting tried by the search: it is rendered and then scored against the target.
sentinel
A check run inside a long job that renders a fixed test now and then and stops the job if the result has drifted, so a corrupted render cannot be scored as real.
separation
Splitting a full mix into instruments so the guitar can be analysed alone.
separator leftovers
Sound from the band (bass, drums, vocals) that the guitar separator fails to remove. It can look like a reverb tail and hide a real one.
session close
The last message a control program sends to the pedal when it is done. Without it the pedal stays in editing mode and its blocks cannot be edited by hand.
slot
A fixed position in the signal chain: drive, amp, cabinet, modulation, delay, reverb, equaliser, in that order. Each slot is optional.
snapshot
One of up to three stored variations of a preset that the pedal can switch between.
specialist
A separate method for one family of effects (gain, linear, dynamics, time-based, modulation) with its own measurements and search, instead of one general method for everything.
stand-in signals
Test audio made by ordinary software effects instead of Helix. Useful for early checks; not proof the method works on Helix.
synthetic-v2
A fresh known-answer set, made with the same generator and the same frozen rules but a new random seed, because the first set has now been seen.
test-retest consistency
How often the listener gives the same answer when a blind pair is repeated. The human's is 7 of 12 across the sessions so far, so small sessions cannot resolve small differences.
time-variant block
An effect that changes its sound on its own over time (for example a free-running chorus), so two renders never cancel exactly.
tone comparison
The loss: the combined measures of how similar two guitar sounds are.
top-1 / top-3
How often the correct answer is the first choice, or among the first three choices.
trust check
A test of whether a measure puts the true preset first among many candidates.
USB slip
A one-sample shift or short burst in the audio coming back from the pedal over its USB connection, which makes a render differ slightly from an identical earlier one.
wall-clock
Real elapsed time on a clock, as opposed to computing time.
